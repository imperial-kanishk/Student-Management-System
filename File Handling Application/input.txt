The current global climate situation has escalated from a looming future threat into an active,
undeniable planetary crisis, marked by record-shattering temperatures and intensifying
ecological volatility. Earth's average surface temperature has risen by approximately
$1.44^\circ\text{C}$ to $1.5^\circ\text{C}$ above pre-industrial baselines,
driven primarily by the ongoing burning of fossil fuels, deforestation, and industrial
emissions that continue to trap greenhouse gases in the atmosphere.

This warming has triggered a cascade of severe, systemic consequences across the globe:
the last decade has been recorded as the hottest in human history, ocean heat content
has reached unprecedented highs, and polar ice sheets and glaciers are melting at alarming rates,
causing sea level rise to accelerate. Consequently, humanity is experiencing a stark increase in
the frequency and severity of extreme weather events, ranging from catastrophic wildfires and
prolonged, multi-year droughts that threaten global food security to intense heatwaves and torrential,
flood-inducing rainfall. Despite widespread international pledges to hit net-zero emissions and cap
global warming at the critical $1.5^\circ\text{C}$ threshold, current policy trajectories show that
emissions have not yet declined fast enough, putting the planet on a path toward temporary
overshoots and potentially permanent disruptions to vital ecosystems.
As climate change increasingly acts as a "threat multiplier"—exacerbating economic inequality,
driving human migration, and straining healthcare systems—the window for a just, rapid transition to
renewable energy and sustainable resource management is narrowing, making the current decade
the most decisive in determining the future habitability of our world.
